Bust Fragment – Roman Villas of Carthage

Home  ›  Archive  ›  Bust Fragment – Roman Villas of Carthage
CarthageRoman Villas of Carthage

This marble bust fragment, discovered in the Roman villas of Carthage, preserves the back of the head and shoulders of a figure. The drapery detail around the neck is still visible, though the face and much of the front are missing due to weathering or loss over time. Busts like this were common in Roman domestic spaces, often used to commemorate ancestors, honor public figures, or serve as decorative art in private villas. Even in its incomplete state, the carving reflects the Roman emphasis on portraiture and individuality. üìå This model is part of the Tanit XR digital preservation initiative, which documents and shares Tunisia’s endangered cultural heritage through 3D scanning and immersive storytelling. 

About Tanit XR

Tanit XR is a nonprofit initiative working to digitally preserve Tunisia’s cultural heritage before it disappears to time, weather, or neglect. Through 3D scanning, an open digital archive, and immersive AR/VR experiences, we make mosaics, statues, and historic sites accessible to students, researchers, and the public everywhere.
