## 3. Smithsonian Cultural Rescue Initiative: a case study for your network

To: culturalrescue@si.edu
Subject: Neapolis after Storm Harry: a volunteer phone-scanning case study for SCRI

Dear SCRI team,

I am Ines Said, founder of Tanit XR, a volunteer community of 85 people on four continents who scan endangered Tunisian heritage objects with their phones and publish them free in 3D, AR and VR. I grew up in Sidi Mahersi, Nabeul, a 15-minute walk from the ruins of Neapolis.

In January 2026, Storm Harry stripped sediment off the coast at Nabeul and exposed parts of Neapolis. Within days our volunteers captured the newly revealed ruins in 3D with their phones, before the sea and the weather took them back. We have more than 100 models published, a weekly community call, and a free course that teaches anyone to capture 3D with a phone.

I think this is a useful case for the people you train: what a community with no budget and no equipment beyond phones can record in the first days after a disaster, what it cannot, and how to organise the volunteers. I would be glad to give a short talk or a hands-on session for your training network, in person in Washington or online, and to share the workflow and the models.

Dr. Laura Harrison, our Chief Scientist and an archaeologist who has worked with us in Tunisia, would join me.

Who would be the right person to talk to? Thank you for the work you do.

Warm regards,
Ines Said
Founder, Tanit XR
