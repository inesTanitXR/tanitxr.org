# Tanit XR × TAYP in Washington, DC. Thursday, October 22, 2026. The plan.

Written 2026-09-19, 33 days out. No venue yet; Laura and Caroline confirmed on September 11; TAYP is the partner. Read the honest part first.

## The honest part

**Cash sponsorship from a company you do not already know, in 33 days, is not realistic.** Corporate sponsorship budgets are decided one to three months ahead, by someone who needs a venue, a date, an attendee number and a sheet in hand before they can say yes. You have the date.

**What is realistic in 33 days, and worth doing:**
1. Everything in kind: a free venue, food and drinks covered by a restaurant or a company, a headset or two on loan, printing. This is how most first community events are "sponsored".
2. One or two "Friend" sponsors at $500 to $1,500, only from people who already know you or TAYP personally. Not cold.
3. A suggested donation at the door ($15 to $25 through Luma or Eventbrite) plus a Donate QR on the screen. Fifty people at $20 covers the drinks and leaves a few hundred dollars.
4. Using the evening to plant the real sponsorships: every company person in the room gets the visit link and a follow-up within 48 hours. The October event is the sales meeting for the 2027 event.

If by **October 8** there is no venue confirmed, do the evening small (a TAYP member's office, a café back room, 30 people, no sponsor) and put the sponsorship effort into a full-day event in spring 2027. Plan B is at the end.

## What the evening is

- **Working title:** "Tunisia in 3D: an evening with Tanit XR and TAYP".
- **Length:** three hours after work, 6:00 to 9:00 pm. Doors and headsets 6:00, talks 6:45 to 7:45, headsets and conversation until 9:00.
- **Programme:** Ines Said, founder, award-winning XR artist (Smithsonian FUTURES installations, Auggie Awards 2026 finalist, NAAEE 30 Under 30, AWE speaker): why a Tunisian developer started scanning Carthage with a phone, and a live walk through Explore in 3D. Dr. Laura Harrison, archaeologist and Chief Scientist: what she saw on the ground in Tunisia and what a phone can and cannot record. Dr. Caroline Nickerson, Partnerships, Executive Director of Florida Community Innovation: the community model and how a room like this one becomes volunteers. Then hands-on: two or three headset stations with the round galleries, phones with the AR view, a "scan something in this room" demo with Nura.
- **Audience:** 60 to 100. TAYP members (Tunisian-American professionals in law, finance, tech, policy), the DC XR and civic-tech crowd, embassy and cultural staff, students from GWU, Georgetown and American, journalists who cover the region.
- **Ask of the room:** join the Thursday call, share the link, donate, and for companies: a fifteen-minute call.

## Week by week

**Week 1, September 19 to 25: the venue and the sheet**
- Lock a venue. Ask in this order (free or near-free for nonprofits): the Embassy of Tunisia cultural space (ask TAYP's board to make the introduction; embassies host diaspora cultural evenings often), the DC Public Library MLK branch (free meeting rooms for nonprofits, book online, Thursday evenings available), Eaton DC (impact hotel with event space, discounts for nonprofits), Halcyon (Georgetown nonprofit hub), a university room through Laura's or Caroline's academic contacts (GWU, Georgetown, American), a TAYP member's employer (law firms and consultancies have event floors and like to host).
- Send TAYP the sponsor sheet (attached) and agree who contacts whom. Ask TAYP for: their member list of companies, their newsletter and Instagram for promotion, one person on the door, and their Luma or Eventbrite account.
- Create the event page (Luma is free and looks good). Suggested donation $20, free for students. Add the visit link and the speakers.

**Week 2, September 26 to October 2: the asks**
- In kind first, ten emails, using the template below. Targets: two Tunisian restaurants or cafés in DC or Arlington for food (in exchange for a credit on the screen and a post), one wine or non-alcoholic drinks sponsor (a TAYP member usually knows one), Meta or Niantic for a headset loan or two Quest 3 units (Ines's Niantic contact first), a print shop for posters and a roll-up banner.
- Friend sponsors, five emails, warm only: employers of TAYP board members, the company of anyone who has said "let me know how I can help" this year, the Florida Community Innovation network (Caroline).
- Announce the event on the site, in the newsletter and on LinkedIn with the visit link. The announcement itself is a sponsorship tool: it shows companies a real audience.

**Week 3, October 3 to 9: confirm or shrink**
- October 8 is the decision date: venue confirmed and at least 40 registrations, or shrink to the small version.
- Confirm speakers' slides, the headset stations (who brings which device, charged, with the tour link), the run of show, the photographer (a volunteer with a good phone is enough), and the follow-up email drafted in advance.

**Week 4, October 10 to 21: fill the room**
- Second announcement, TAYP reminder, personal invitations from Laura and Caroline to their DC contacts, a post from Ines with the 3D card.
- Print the QR posters: one for the visit, one for Donate, one for the volunteer form.
- Sponsor logos on the screen loop, the thank-you post drafted.

**October 22: the evening**
- Run of show below. One person owns the door and the sign-in sheet (name, email, company, "would you like a call?").

**October 23 to 24: the 48-hour follow-up**
- Thank-you post tagging sponsors, the venue and TAYP, with the photo of people in headsets.
- Every company person who ticked "yes" gets a personal email with the visit link and one concrete offer from the partners page, and a fifteen-minute call proposed for the following week. This is where the first real sponsor comes from.

## Plan B: a full day or a weekend in spring 2027

If the evening stays small, aim the sponsorship effort at something companies can plan for: "Tunisia in 3D Day" in DC, April or May 2027, a Saturday.
- **Runway:** five months. Sponsor asks go out in December with a confirmed venue and date; decisions land in January and February.
- **Shape:** morning workshop (learn phone capture, 40 people), lunch, afternoon talks and panel (200 people), evening reception with headsets and the round galleries on a big screen. Hackathon track on the Sunday with our published scans if a university partner joins.
- **Budget to raise:** $15,000 to $25,000: venue, food, travel for two speakers from Tunisia, headsets, printing, a videographer, and a modest fee for local volunteers.
- **Sponsor levels:** Friend $1,000, Partner $5,000 (a workshop for your team, logo everywhere), Presenting $15,000 (naming, a gallery in the museum, a talk at your office).
- **Why companies say yes to this and not to an evening:** a full day has an agenda they can put their people into, a photo opportunity worth a press release, and a lead time that fits their budget cycle.
- The October evening is where you find them: every card collected on the 22nd goes into the spring list.
