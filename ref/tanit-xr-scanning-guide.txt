 Tanit XR Scanning Guide 
[h2] Using Scaniverse to Preserve Heritage 
[h3] 📲 Getting Started 
[li] App : Download Scaniverse from the iOS App Store (iPhone 12 Pro or newer recommended for LiDAR support). 
[li] Goal : Create detailed 3D scans of historical landmarks, ruins, pottery, architecture, and cultural artifacts for a global digital heritage archive. 
[h2] 🧭 Choosing What to Scan 
[p] Not sure where to start? Look for objects, places, and details that carry cultural, historical, artistic, or community meaning. 
[p] Good things to scan include: 
[li] Architecture and ruins — doors, arches, columns, facades, walls, courtyards, tombs, monuments, and historic homes 
[li] Objects and artifacts — pottery, tools, carvings, statues, tiles, jewelry, textiles, inscriptions, and household items 
[li] Small details — patterns, textures, symbols, damage, repairs, maker’s marks, or decorative elements 
[li] Everyday heritage — bakeries, workshops, markets, gathering places, family heirlooms, gardens, and community spaces 
[li] At-risk heritage — places or objects threatened by weather, neglect, development, conflict, theft, or loss of memory 
[p] Before scanning, ask: 
[li] What story does this object or place tell? 
[li] Who uses it, remembers it, or cares about it? 
[li] Is it connected to a tradition, craft, family, neighborhood, or historic event? 
[li] Is it changing, disappearing, or at risk? 
[p] Please do not scan sacred, private, restricted, or sensitive objects without permission. When in doubt, ask a local caretaker, community member, owner, or cultural authority first. 
[h2] 🕯️ Scanning Intangible Heritage 
[p] Some heritage is not just a building or object. It lives in stories, songs, rituals, recipes, crafts, dances, languages, memories, and everyday practices. This is called intangible heritage . 
[p] You cannot always 3D scan intangible heritage directly, but you can document the objects, spaces, and people connected to it. 
[p] Examples: 
[li] A traditional bread recipe → scan the oven, tools, table, or bakery space 
[li] A weaving practice → scan the loom, textile patterns, tools, or finished pieces 
[li] A family story → scan the home, courtyard, photograph, object, or place connected to the memory 
[li] A festival or ritual → scan decorations, costumes, instruments, gathering spaces, or symbolic objects 
[li] A disappearing craft → scan the tools, workshop, materials, and finished work 
[p] When documenting intangible heritage, include context with your upload: 
[li] What is this tradition, story, or practice called? 
[li] Who practices it? 
[li] Where does it happen? 
[li] How did you learn about it? 
[li] Why does it matter? 
[li] Is there a story, memory, or quote that should go with the scan? 
[p] Always get permission before recording people, private spaces, ceremonies, sacred practices, or personal stories. Tanit XR is not just preserving objects — we are preserving the worlds, memories, and meanings around them. 
[h3] 🧱 Step-by-Step Scanning Instructions 
[h4] 1. Open Scaniverse 
[li] Tap the “+” button to start a new scan. 
[li] Choose “Mesh” (not “Splat”) — this is what we need for Tanit XR. 
[li] Select the size of your object:
[li] Small Object – pottery, carvings, statues 
[li] Medium Object – doors, columns, mosaics 
[li] Large Area – facades, walls, monuments 
[h4] 2. Begin the Scan 
[li] Move slowly around the object while recording a video . 
[li] Get multiple angles : walk around, crouch down, raise your phone, etc. 
[li] Avoid fast movements and make sure to capture all sides . 
[li] In bright sun , try to scan in partial shade or overcast light. 
[h4] 3. Save Without Processing (Important!) 
[li] If you’re outside and don’t have strong Wi-Fi or data, tap “Save to process later.” 
[li] Processing uses a lot of data — it’s best to wait until you’re home with Wi-Fi . 
[h3] 🗂️ Processing and Exporting 
[h4] 4. Back at Home: Process Your Scan 
[li] Open the Scaniverse Library (bottom menu). 
[li] Tap your saved scan. 
[li] Tap the name and give it a clear title (e.g., “Ksar Ouled Soltane – Main Door”). 
[li] Tap “Process” and wait for the app to complete the 3D model. 
[h4] 5. Export the Model 
[li] Once processed, tap “Share” > “Export Model.” 
[li] Select FBX format. 
[li] Keep textures enabled . 
[li] Name it “ Scan Title_Location “ 
[h4] 6. Share Your Model (upload your file, below, and then email description and other details to info@tanitxr.org)

[h3] ✅ Tips for Great Scans 
[li] Scan slowly and steadily 
[li] Avoid people or shadows in your scan 
[li] Focus on texture and angles — walk around the object fully 
[li] Natural daylight is good, but harsh sun causes glare – avoid scanning at noon
